# EU Regulations and Directives — Digest for CISO Skills

The six instruments below set the regulatory backbone of the GAISO plugin. Each skill in `skills/` cites specific articles from this list. When you draft an OVB, use these as the canonical references.

Source: GAISO task T3.1, "Organisational and technical standards, legal basis for GenAI-driven cybersecurity". Reproduced in English from the Lithuanian deliverable.

## 1. NIS2 Directive — Directive (EU) 2022/2555

**Full title**: Directive of the European Parliament and of the Council of 14 December 2022 on measures for a high common level of cybersecurity across the Union, amending Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 and Directive (EU) 2018/1972 and repealing Directive (EU) 2016/1148.

- **Scope**: important and essential entities in the sectors of NIS2 Annex I (high criticality) and Annex II.
- **Risk-management duties**: Art. 21 — ten minimum measures covering risk analysis, incident handling, business continuity, supply chain, acquisition and development, effectiveness testing, basic cyber hygiene and training, cryptography, HR security, access control, MFA.
- **Governance**: Art. 20 — management bodies' approval of cyber-risk measures and mandatory training.
- **Incident notification**: Art. 23 — early warning within 24h of awareness, notification within 72h, final report within one month.
- **Implementing acts**: Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690 of 17 October 2024 — technical and methodological requirements for risk-management measures and significance thresholds for specific sector providers (DNS, TLD, cloud, data centre, CDN, managed service/security providers, marketplaces, search engines, social networks, trust services).
- **Guidance**: Commission Communication of 13 September 2023 — guidelines on the application of NIS2 Art. 4(1)–(2).

Most-used articles in this plugin: **Art. 20, 21, 23**; the Annex of Implementing Reg. 2024/2690 for significance.

## 2. Cybersecurity Act — Regulation (EU) 2019/881

**Full title**: Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council of 17 April 2019 on ENISA and on information and communication technology cybersecurity certification.

- Gives ENISA a permanent mandate and establishes the European Cybersecurity Certification Framework.
- Relevant to skills that handle product or service certification claims, or when a supplier's ENISA-certified status is part of a risk judgment.
- Informs interpretation of "state of the art" used across NIS2 Art. 21.

Most-used: **Art. 54 (certification scheme content), Art. 59 (information), ENISA common criteria**.

## 3. AI Act — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689

**Full title**: Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 June 2024 laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence.

- **Scope of this plugin**: The GAISO assistant itself is a general-purpose AI system integrated into a high-risk cybersecurity function. Obligations therefore include:
  - Art. 4 — AI literacy of staff using the system.
  - Art. 9 — Risk-management system covering the AI system's lifecycle.
  - Art. 10 — Data governance, including bias and quality of training and evaluation data.
  - Art. 13 — Transparency and information to deployers.
  - Art. 14 — Human oversight.
  - Art. 15 — Accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity of the AI system.
  - Art. 26 — Deployer obligations for high-risk systems.
  - Art. 50 — Transparency obligations for content generated by AI (relevant to F8 detection, and to any CISO output shown externally).
  - Art. 73 — Incident reporting for high-risk AI systems.

Most-used: **Art. 4, 9, 10, 13, 14, 15, 26, 50, 73**.

## 4. GDPR — Regulation (EU) 2016/679

**Full title**: Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council of 27 April 2016 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, repealing Directive 95/46/EC.

- **Cross-cutting**: any log, alert, ticket, incident record, or training scenario that includes personal data is in GDPR scope.
- Most-used articles:
  - Art. 5 — principles (purpose limitation, data minimisation, integrity and confidentiality).
  - Art. 15–22 — data-subject rights procedures.
  - Art. 24 — controller responsibility.
  - Art. 25 — privacy by design and by default.
  - Art. 28 — processor / supplier agreements.
  - Art. 32 — security of processing.
  - Art. 33–34 — personal-data breach notification (72h to the supervisory authority, without undue delay to data subjects).
  - Art. 35 — DPIA.
  - Art. 39 — DPO tasks.

Interplay with NIS2: both can apply to the same incident. The GDPR clock starts on awareness of a personal-data breach; it can start earlier than a NIS2 "significant incident" determination.

## 5. CER Directive — Directive (EU) 2022/2557

**Full title**: Directive of the European Parliament and of the Council of 14 December 2022 on the resilience of critical entities, repealing Council Directive 2008/114/EC.

- **Scope**: resilience obligations for critical entities in eleven sectors.
- **Art. 11** — risk assessment.
- **Art. 13** — resilience measures, including physical, organisational and technical measures, business continuity, personnel vetting.
- **Complements NIS2**: NIS2 targets cybersecurity; CER targets broader resilience (physical, process, supply-chain). Most NIS2 essential entities are also CER critical entities.

## 6. Cyber Resilience Act — Regulation (EU) 2024/2847

**Full title**: Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council of 23 October 2024 on horizontal cybersecurity requirements for products with digital elements.

- **Scope**: manufacturers, importers, distributors of products with digital elements placed on the EU market.
- **Art. 13** — essential cybersecurity requirements (Annex I).
- **Art. 14** — vulnerability handling requirements.
- **Reporting**: exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents to ENISA and national CSIRTs.
- **Interplay**: for a CISO of a critical-infrastructure operator, CRA shows up through procurement and supplier management. For a CISO of a manufacturer, CRA is the primary product regime.

## 7. Out of scope (cited but not mapped here)

The following instruments are intentionally out of the GAISO T3.1 horizontal mapping and are not covered by per-control tables:

- DORA — Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (financial entities).
- Cyber Solidarity Act.
- Digital Services Act.
- Digital Markets Act.
- Chips Act.
- ePrivacy Directive.
- Data Governance Act, Data Act.
- Product Liability Directive revision.
- Sector-specific acts in NIS2 Annex I / II.

Skills operating in these regimes must add the relevant articles to the OVB and cite them explicitly.

## 8. Citation conventions for this plugin

- Always cite the act and a specific article. "NIS2" alone is not a citation; "NIS2 Art. 21(2)(b)" is.
- When a duty crosses multiple instruments (e.g., incident with personal data in an essential entity), cite both (e.g., "NIS2 Art. 23 + GDPR Art. 33").
- When citing the AI Act with respect to the CISO assistant itself (not the supported entity), name the article and indicate "for the assistant" or "for the deployer" to disambiguate.
